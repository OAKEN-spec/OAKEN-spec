01 / A name, not a number

A name is the shortest contract you can write.

It says: this address is me. The registry stores it, your resolver answers it, and your wallet holds it. Nothing here depends on OAKEN staying online — the name is an NFT in your wallet, and anyone who can read the chain can read it.

What this is not. OAKEN does not issue .hood names, cannot freeze them, and cannot recover one for you. The registry is HD Protocol's contract on Robinhood Chain (chain 4663). A name is an NFT: whoever holds it can move or sell it. If you register one and stop at step 2, it exists but nothing resolves to it — that is the state most names on this chain are in. Verification is not an audit, and a name is not a guarantee of identity.

What this is not

OAKEN does not issue .sns names, cannot freeze them, and cannot recover one for you. The registry is SNS's program on Solana; the price is theirs and OAKEN takes $0. .sns is the current TLD; .sol is the legacy one and may resolve elsewhere or not at all while SNS migrates it. A name is an account owned by a wallet: whoever holds it can move it. A name is not a guarantee of identity.
